Save, return, and submit

Automatic save: Work saves in this browser on this device when storage is available. It does not sync across devices or submit to your instructor. Private browsing or clearing browser data may remove it.

At the end of a work session: Use “Save my work backup.” Keep the downloaded .json file. To resume on another device, use “Open a previously saved appraisal backup” at the bottom.

When Canvas asks for a submission: Export the current PDF. You do not need a PDF after every session. A CSV downloads your running evidence table for Excel or Google Sheets; it is not your resumable backup.

Start here: how to use this workspace, with examples
  1. Keep your full-text article and course reference page open beside this tool.
  2. Enter your name and question, then add a source. Open its numbered sections in order.
  3. Copy short exact source excerpts and identify where they appear. Write your interpretations in your own words.
  4. Save a checkpoint to receive self-check prompts. Your record stays editable. Explain meaningful revisions below; add instructor feedback later when it is available.
  5. Return after peer and instructor feedback. Save a backup regularly, then export the PDF required by Canvas.

Fictional example of an evidence locator: Methods, Participants subsection, paragraph 2. For a result: Table 2, walking-distance row, intervention and comparison columns.

Fictional example of a specific concern: “The Methods state that participants volunteered from one university. Their activity habits may differ from the older adults in my question, so applicability is uncertain.”

Fictional example of a revision: “I initially described the finding as proof of an effect. After checking the observational design, I changed it to an association.”

These examples illustrate the form of a response. Use evidence from your own sources. Short, specific answers are sufficient; longer responses do not earn extra credit.

My running evidence table

Your current article entries appear here automatically. The PICO in each row describes the source, which may differ from your own research question. Edit the article record below to update its row.

Scroll horizontally to compare columns. Blank fields are labeled Not recorded. Review rows for differences in populations, measures, and findings rather than treating all studies as interchangeable.

Open the CSV in Excel or import it into Google Sheets. This is a downloaded copy; edits there do not update the interactive.

My article records

Build concise records for your evidence set. Use the most specific design supported by the source. A systematic review needs review-method evidence; a position stand needs evidence-development and recommendation-process evidence.

The final working set requires at least four primary studies and one systematic review, with or without meta-analysis. Use a position stand as an alternative only when your assignment allows it.
